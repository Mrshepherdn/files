# How 3 Friends Can Start a Supported Living Company With £1,000 Each

**Meta Description:** You don't need £50,000 to start a care business. Here's how three people can pool resources, get funded, and own a supported living company within days.

**Target Keywords:** start a care business with no money, group business ownership, start business with friends, affordable care business, supported living startup cost

---

What if starting a business didn't require a huge amount of money? What if three people — each putting in less than a week's wages — could own a fully operational care company within days?

This isn't hypothetical. This is exactly how it works.

## The Setup

Three people. Each has around £1,000 to contribute. Between them, they have £3,000.

A turnkey supported living venture — fully registered, fully documented, with every policy, procedure, financial model, business plan, website, email, and phone system included — costs £5,000.

They're £2,000 short. Or are they?

## Route 1: Pure Pooling

Each person contributes £1,667 instead of £1,000. That's the entire venture cost covered. No borrowing. No interest. No applications.

For many people, the difference between £1,000 and £1,667 is one extra month of saving. For others, it might mean selling something they don't need, picking up a few extra shifts, or simply adjusting the timeline by a few weeks.

**Total personal cost: £1,667 each. Business owned outright.**

## Route 2: Pooling + Start Up Loans

Each person contributes £1,000 (£3,000 total) to cover part of the venture cost. Then each applies individually for a Start Up Loan to cover the remaining £2,000 — plus working capital for launch.

Start Up Loans are:
- Government-backed through the British Business Bank
- Unsecured (no collateral)
- 6% fixed interest
- Available up to £25,000 per person
- Available to each individual in the group

So three people, each borrowing just £5,000-£8,000, gives the group:
- £3,000 in personal contributions (venture cost)
- £15,000-£24,000 in combined Start Up Loans (remaining venture cost + working capital)

That's a fully funded business with working capital to cover initial operating costs — recruitment, training, premises setup — before revenue starts.

**Total personal cost: £1,000 each. Total accessible funding: up to £78,000.**

## Route 3: Zero Personal Capital

Each person applies for a Start Up Loan individually. No personal contribution required — the loans cover the venture cost and working capital entirely.

Three people. Three individual loan applications. Three approvals based on the business plan and financial projections that come with the venture.

**Total personal cost: £0 each. Business ownership: immediate.**

## How Ownership Works

When three people own a business together, clarity is everything. The venture comes with governance documentation that defines:

- **Share allocation** — who owns what percentage
- **Director appointments** — who is formally registered at Companies House
- **Decision-making framework** — how major decisions are made (majority vote, unanimous, etc.)
- **Role definitions** — who does what in the day-to-day operation
- **Exit provisions** — what happens if someone wants to leave
- **Dividend policy** — how profits are distributed

This isn't a handshake deal. It's a **structured corporate arrangement** with legal documentation. Everyone knows their position, their responsibilities, and their entitlements from day one.

## Who Brings What

The beauty of a three-person team is that each person can bring different strengths:

**Person A: The Care Expert**
A support worker or nurse with hands-on care experience. They understand service delivery, CQC requirements, and what good care looks like. They might take the lead on care operations and quality.

**Person B: The Organiser**
Someone with administration, management, or coordination skills. They handle recruitment, HR, scheduling, and business operations. They follow the procedures and keep the operation running smoothly.

**Person C: The Networker**
Someone who's good with people — building relationships with local authorities, housing providers, referral sources, and families. They focus on business development and securing placements.

Or maybe all three have care experience and they share everything equally. The framework supports both models. The policies and procedures don't require any specific person to do any specific thing — they tell **whoever is doing the task** how to do it correctly.

## What About Risk?

Every business carries risk. The question is whether the risk is **managed.**

In a turnkey venture with complete policies, procedures, and financial frameworks:

- **Regulatory risk** is managed by CQC-aligned documentation and governance
- **Financial risk** is managed by stress-tested projections and sensitivity analysis
- **Operational risk** is managed by procedures that cover every scenario
- **People risk** is managed by HR policies, supervision frameworks, and clear role definitions

Three people also **distribute risk.** If one person is unwell, the business doesn't stop. If one person has a personal issue, the others maintain operations. The business is more resilient with shared ownership than with a sole operator.
