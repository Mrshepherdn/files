## Section 2: Three Ways to Own — All Legal

### Model 1: Own It. Don't Work In It.

You buy a supported living venture. You're registered as shareholder and director. You hire a Registered Manager and staff to run the business. You oversee governance — finances, strategy, quality — but you don't do operational work.

**You keep your sponsored job.** The company runs. You receive dividends.

Owning shares and receiving dividends is not "work" under immigration law. Director oversight (governance, not operations) is ownership activity, not employment.

**Your income:**
- Visa salary: £24,000 - £32,000
- Business dividends (Year 2): £48,889
- **Combined: up to £80,889**

### Model 2: Own It. Work 20 Hours Per Week.

Skilled Worker visa holders can do **supplementary employment** of up to 20 hours per week — outside the hours of your sponsored job — if it's in the same occupation code or on the Shortage Occupation List.

Healthcare roles are widely represented on the Shortage Occupation List. This means you could potentially:
- Work your sponsored job as normal
- Spend 20 hours per week in YOUR business — managing, overseeing, building
- Earn your salary AND grow your company

**Same week. Two income streams. Both legal.**

### Model 3: Full Ownership After ILR

After 5 years on a Skilled Worker visa, you can apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain. Once granted, you have **no restrictions on work or business activity.**

Many people use Models 1 or 2 now — building while they wait — and then go full-time in their business after ILR. By then, the company has revenue, clients, and staff. You step into a running operation, not a startup.

## Section 9: FAQ

**Will this affect my Skilled Worker visa?**
Owning shares and receiving dividends does not constitute "work" under immigration law. However, we strongly recommend consulting a qualified immigration adviser about your specific circumstances before taking any action. The supplementary employment rules (20 hours) depend on your occupation code and visa conditions.

**Do I need to tell my employer?**
You are not legally required to inform your employer that you own shares in a company. Check your employment contract for any restrictive clauses (such as competing business restrictions), but company ownership itself is a private matter.

**Can I be the Registered Manager of my own company?**
If you have the right qualifications (typically Level 5 in Leadership for Health and Social Care) and can meet CQC fitness requirements, potentially yes — but this would constitute work, so the supplementary employment rules apply. Many Skilled Worker visa holders hire a Registered Manager instead.

**What about tax?**
Dividend income is taxed in the UK. You will need to complete a Self Assessment tax return if you receive dividends above your personal dividend allowance. We recommend consulting a UK accountant. The good news: dividends are taxed at lower rates than employment income.

**Can I send money home from the business?**
Dividends paid to you are your money. What you do with your personal income — including international transfers — is entirely your choice.

**What happens when I get ILR?**
All restrictions lift. You can work full-time in your business, leave your sponsored job, expand, or do whatever you choose. If you started the business while on your visa, you step into an already-running operation.

**Is this really legal?**
Yes. This is standard UK company law. Any person, of any nationality, can own shares in and be a director of a UK limited company. We include an immigration disclaimer because individual visa conditions vary, but the fundamental right to own a company is clear and well-established.
