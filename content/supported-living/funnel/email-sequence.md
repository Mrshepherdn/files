# Supported Living Venture — Email Funnel Sequence

## Overview

This email sequence is triggered when a prospect engages with a landing page — either by downloading a guide, requesting information, or submitting a contact form. The same structure applies to all audience segments, but the **language, examples, and emphasis change** based on which landing page they came from.

**Sequence length:** 7 emails over 14 days
**Goal:** Move prospect from interest to consultation booking

## Email 2: Day 2 — The Framework Explained

**Subject Line Options:**
- "How policies and procedures run a business for you"
- "The document that answers every question a manager could ask"
- "Why you don't need to 'know everything' before you start"

---

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Yesterday we said you don't need experience to own a business. Today we'll explain why.

Every business has an operating framework — a set of documents that define how everything works. In supported living, that framework is built on **policies and procedures.**

**A policy** is the position on a particular subject. It's the WHAT.
**A procedure** is the application of that policy. It's the HOW.

For example:

A complaint comes in. What do you do?

The **complaints policy** says: "We welcome complaints as opportunities to improve. All complaints are investigated and resolved within 28 days."

The **complaints procedure** says: "Step 1: Log the complaint. Step 2: Acknowledge within 2 days. Step 3: Assign an investigator. Step 4: Investigate within 14 days..." — and so on, step by step.

**Every question has already been answered.** Recruitment, safeguarding, medication, data protection, staff supervision — there's a policy and procedure for all of it.

This means:
- You don't wonder what to do. You look it up.
- You don't need years of experience. You need commitment to follow the framework.
- The operations themselves become your teacher.

Every Zundara supported living venture comes with a **complete policy and procedure library** — written, CQC-aligned, and ready from day one.

Tomorrow we'll talk about the money.

The Zundara Team

## Email 4: Day 6 — The Numbers

**Subject Line Options:**
- "What a supported living business actually earns"
- "Year 2: £334,800 revenue. Here's how."
- "The financial breakdown — no surprises"

---

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

We've talked about frameworks and funding. Now let's talk about what the business actually generates.

Here are the projected financials for a supported living venture:

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | £201,600 | £334,800 |
| Operating Costs | £168,000 | £285,911 |
| Net Profit | £33,600 | £48,889 |
| Owner-Operator Income | — | £81,950 |

**Where does the revenue come from?**

Local authorities commission supported living services and pay providers weekly or monthly per client. Rates vary by area and needs level, but typical rates range from £15-£25+ per hour per client. With multiple clients receiving varying hours of support, revenue builds predictably.

**What are the main costs?**

Staff wages are your biggest cost — support workers, a Registered Manager, and potentially senior support workers. Then premises costs, insurance, training, and administration. All of these are modelled in the financial projections that come with your venture.

**What does "owner-operator income" mean?**

If you work in the business yourself — as director and possibly Registered Manager — your income combines your salary (a business expense) plus profit distribution. That's how Year 2 income reaches £81,950.

**What if three of you own it?**

Profit splits three ways. But all three of you can draw salaries if you work in the business. And as you grow — more clients, more properties — the numbers multiply.

These are conservative projections. The financial models include sensitivity analysis showing best case, expected, and conservative scenarios.

Best,
The Zundara Team
